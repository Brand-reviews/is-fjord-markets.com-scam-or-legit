Fjord Markets Review 2026: Is Fjord-Markets.com Legit or High Risk?

Published by BrandsCheck | Broker Investigation | Updated October 2026

Our preliminary assessment: Exercise caution.

Fjord Markets presents itself as a multi-asset trading broker offering forex, equities, cryptocurrencies, commodities and indices. However, its regulatory claims and important compliance information need independent verification. We recommend withholding deposits until the legal entity, licence and client-fund protections have been confirmed.

In this Fjord Markets review, we examine fjord-markets.com, focusing on its claimed regulation, account requirements, leverage, compliance documentation and transparency. Our objective is to help traders identify unanswered questions before committing money to an online broker.

1. What Is Fjord Markets?

Fjord Markets describes itself as a multi-asset brokerage providing access to different financial markets through one trading platform. Its website advertises execution speeds, platform uptime, thousands of instruments and service in multiple countries.

These figures and service descriptions are claims made by the company. We have not independently verified the reported execution performance, uptime, client numbers or trading infrastructure.

The website also presents a company history beginning in Bergen, Norway, and lists several people as members of its management team. Before relying on this information, prospective clients should independently establish the operating company's legal name, registration number, physical presence and responsible directors.

2. Fjord Markets Regulation and FINMA Claims

Important regulatory concern:

Fjord Markets has a page stating that it is supervised by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) and has been licensed since 2016. However, the page does not display a specific licence number or full legal entity details alongside the claim.

Regulatory statements on a broker's own website are not sufficient proof of authorisation. A credible verification process requires matching the broker's legal entity, registration details and permitted financial activities against the relevant regulator's official records.

FINMA is Switzerland's financial-market regulator. Before trusting the claim, readers should:

  • Ask Fjord Markets for its complete registered legal name and FINMA authorisation number.
  • Search FINMA's official database independently at FINMA's authorised institutions directory.
  • Confirm that the entity listed by the regulator is the same company responsible for fjord-markets.com.
  • Check whether the authorisation covers the specific brokerage and investment services being advertised.
  • Contact the regulator through contact details published on its official website if the information remains unclear.

We have not established from the available evidence that Fjord Markets is authorised by FINMA. Equally, the absence of a licence number on its website alone does not establish that the company is fraudulent. Until the claim is independently confirmed, it should be treated as unverified.

3. Compliance Documentation Raises Questions

Fjord Markets' compliance page describes policies for client money, anti-money laundering, order execution, conflicts of interest and complaints handling.

A material documentation concern

The publicly indexed compliance page includes the statement: “Placeholder text. This document must be reviewed and replaced by a qualified lawyer before the site goes live.” This raises questions about whether the compliance documentation has been fully reviewed and finalised.

The same page states that client funds are held in segregated accounts with tier-one banking partners. That is a claim that requires evidence. Traders should request the identity of the legal entity holding their funds, the relevant account arrangements and the contractual protections that apply if the broker fails.

Read the company's compliance page directly and compare its statements with the binding terms provided during account opening.

4. Account Tiers, Deposits and Leverage

The broker advertises six account tiers, with entry requirements increasing substantially as clients move to higher levels.

  • Starter: €250–€1,000, with advertised leverage up to 1:100.
  • Growth: €4,500, with advertised leverage up to 1:150.
  • Pro: €14,500, with advertised leverage up to 1:200.
  • Elite: €50,000, with advertised leverage up to 1:300.
  • Vault: €100,000, with advertised leverage up to 1:400.
  • Private Banking: €500,000, with advertised leverage up to 1:700.

These figures are taken from the broker's published account information and have not been independently confirmed as the terms of an actual trading account.

Higher leverage magnifies both profits and losses. At 1:100 leverage, a relatively small market movement can have a substantial effect on the funds committed to a position. Leverage of 1:700 creates particularly significant exposure and may not be permitted for all retail clients in regulated jurisdictions.

The website also advertises “capital support” or investment support for certain account tiers, including percentages as high as 100% for its Private Banking tier. The precise meaning, conditions and financial obligations associated with these offers should be established in writing before a client considers depositing.

Do not assume that a larger deposit guarantees better returns, safer trading or preferential withdrawal treatment.

5. Trading Platform and Performance Claims

Fjord Markets promotes its own trading terminal, cross-asset watchlists, market-depth tools, risk controls, trade journals and API access for higher-tier accounts.

Its website also advertises an 18-millisecond median execution time, more than 3,400 tradable instruments and 99.98% platform uptime. These are marketing claims rather than independently audited results in the evidence reviewed for this article.

Before funding an account, ask for clear information about:

  • Who operates the trading platform and executes client orders.
  • Whether the displayed prices are live and independently sourced.
  • How spreads, commissions, swaps and slippage are calculated.
  • Whether trade confirmations and account statements can be independently reconciled.
  • What happens to open positions and client balances if the platform becomes unavailable.

A professional-looking interface or detailed technical language is not, by itself, proof that a brokerage is properly regulated or financially sound.

6. Contact Information and Transparency

The website lists a Swiss telephone number, an address in Bergen, Norway, and the email address support@fjord-markets.email. It also states that its website contact form is not connected to a backend and that submissions are not stored.

These details do not independently establish whether the company is legitimate. However, the use of different country references makes it especially important to identify the precise legal entity that would receive and hold a client's funds.

Before proceeding, request written answers to the following:

  • What is the full legal name and company registration number?
  • Which country legally governs the brokerage agreement?
  • Which regulator authorises the company, and what is its licence number?
  • Which legal entity receives deposits and processes withdrawals?
  • What are the applicable withdrawal fees, processing times and dispute procedures?

Keep copies of emails, agreements, deposit instructions and any representations about regulation or expected returns.

7. Is Fjord Markets a Scam?

Based on the information reviewed for this article, we cannot responsibly conclude that Fjord Markets is definitively a scam. That conclusion would require stronger evidence than the concerns identified here.

Nevertheless, there are significant unanswered questions that prospective clients should resolve before placing money with the platform:

  • The claimed FINMA authorisation has not been independently established in this review.
  • The compliance page contains an explicit placeholder warning.
  • The website advertises very high leverage and substantial account-tier deposits.
  • Claims about segregated client funds and trading performance have not been independently verified.
  • The contact page says its contact form is not connected to a backend.
Our recommendation

Treat Fjord Markets as an unverified, higher-risk option until its regulatory status, legal entity, client-money arrangements and contractual withdrawal terms have been confirmed through independent sources. Do not deposit money simply because a representative claims the company is licensed or promises special trading benefits.

8. What to Do Before Depositing

Use this checklist before opening or funding an account:

  • Verify the legal entity and licence directly with the named regulator.
  • Read the complete client agreement, risk disclosure and withdrawal policy.
  • Confirm the identity of the recipient before sending funds.
  • Never pay an unexpected “tax”, “unlocking fee” or additional deposit to release a withdrawal without independently verifying the legal basis.
  • Avoid borrowing money or depositing funds needed for essential expenses.
  • Keep records of all transactions and communications.

If you have already deposited and encounter withdrawal problems, preserve your records and contact your bank, card issuer or payment provider promptly to ask what recovery or dispute options may be available. You may also report the matter to the relevant financial regulator or law-enforcement authority.
